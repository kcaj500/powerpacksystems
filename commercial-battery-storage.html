Where battery storage can help

Storage lets you use energy at a different time from when it is generated or purchased. For a business with surplus solar, that can mean using more of its own generation later. With a suitable tariff, it can also mean charging during lower-cost periods and using stored energy when electricity is more expensive.

Battery storage can support demand management where equipment creates short peaks in electricity use. Backup for defined critical loads can also be designed into a project; the required resilience and operating arrangements form part of the specification.

Designed around the numbers

We review consumption, tariffs, solar generation and grid constraints before proposing a system. For established sites, the previous 12 months of half-hourly electricity data is ideal. New sites can start with projected demand, planned equipment and operating hours.

Potential savings depend on the tariff, charging strategy, system losses and how the site operates. A proposal sets out the equipment, costs and assumptions so you can assess the business case.

Common project questions

Do we need solar panels?

It depends on your goal. For our bill-saving solutions, yes — we combine new or existing solar with battery storage to help you use more of your own generation. For managing grid constraints or providing UPS and backup power, solar is not necessarily required. We design the system around what you need it to achieve.

Will a battery lower our electricity bills?

Yes, with a system and operating strategy suited to your site. The amount you can save depends on electricity prices, usable storage, system losses and the timing of demand.

Can it keep our business running during a power cut?

A system can be designed for defined critical loads. The required power, backup duration and changeover arrangements must be agreed during design; storage alone does not establish a backup specification.
